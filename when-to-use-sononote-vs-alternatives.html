Sono Note · Is it right for you?

When should you use Sono Note?

Use Sono Note when you record a meeting, lecture, client call or quick idea and want a clean summary, key points and an action-item list instead of a wall of raw transcript. It can also draft a follow-up email in the tone you choose, and the original recording stays in the note so you can replay and check it. Your notes live on your iPhone with no account, no ads and no tracking.

Written by the developer of Sono Note. We tried to keep this fair: the table below only lists what each app's own US App Store listing says.

Sono Note works well if you…

  • Want a usable note, not just a transcript: a summary, key points, an action-item list and an optional follow-up email draft.
  • Record meetings, client calls, classes or interviews. Ready-made templates for these (and for doctor visits, ideas, support cases and project recaps) aim for a useful result on the first try.
  • Want recordings and notes to stay on your iPhone. There is no account, and the developer runs no servers of its own. Transcription uses Apple's speech recognition, which may send audio to Apple for some languages; Local Only mode keeps everything on your device, even in airplane mode.
  • Prefer one purchase to a subscription. Recording and transcription are free with no daily limit (up to 3 hours per recording), the free plan includes 2 AI summaries a day, and Pro is a single one-time purchase.
  • Need to export: text, Markdown or PDF (PDF export is part of Pro).

Another app may suit you better if you…

  • Want several people to view and edit the same meeting notes, or a web version. Otter's listing describes group editing, shared links and a web app.
  • Mainly need a recorder with audio editing, such as trimming or reducing background noise. Apple's Voice Memos lists trim, replace and Enhance Recording.
  • Record conversations with several speakers and want each speaker labeled. Otter's listing describes tagging speakers; Sono Note's listing says the clearest results come from clear, single-speaker recordings.

Based on their US App Store listings: Sono Note turns a recording into a summary, key points, action items and a follow-up email draft and keeps notes on your iPhone. Voice Memos is Apple's recorder with editing tools, iCloud sync and English transcripts on iPhone 12 and later. Otter transcribes in real time in four languages and lists automated meeting notes and group editing. Just Press Record is a one-tap recorder that transcribes in over 30 languages and runs on Apple Watch and Mac.

Frequently asked questions

When should I use Sono Note?

Use Sono Note when you want a recording of a meeting, lecture, client call or quick idea turned into a clean summary, key points and an action-item list, with the original audio kept in the note so you can replay and check it. It's also a good fit if you don't want to create an account and want your notes to live on your iPhone.

Is Sono Note free to use?

Recording and transcription are free for everyone, with no daily limit, and each recording can be up to 3 hours long. The free plan includes 2 AI summaries a day, and every recording and transcript is saved so you can summarize it later. Pro is a single one-time purchase with no subscription and adds unlimited AI summaries, PDF export, advanced templates, custom vocabulary, every follow-up email tone and full backup and restore.

Does Sono Note upload my recordings?

Your notes live on your iPhone, there is no account, and the developer runs no servers of its own, so nothing is uploaded to the developer. Transcription uses Apple's speech recognition, which may send audio to Apple for some languages. If you choose Local Only mode, everything stays on your device, even in airplane mode.

Should I use Sono Note or Apple's Voice Memos?

Voice Memos is a recorder with editing tools such as trim, replace and Enhance Recording, iCloud sync and Apple Watch recording, and its App Store listing says transcripts are available on iPhone 12 and later in English. Choose Sono Note if you also want a summary, key points, action items and a follow-up email draft from the recording.

Should I use Sono Note or Otter for team meetings?

Otter's App Store listing describes automated meeting notes for Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams, inviting group members to view and edit notes, and a web version. If several people need to work on the same notes, that fits Otter's listing. Sono Note is built around notes that stay on your own iPhone with no account.

Does Sono Note work well with several speakers?

The App Store listing says the clearest results come from clear, single-speaker recordings, and that generated text is meant to be reviewed before use.
